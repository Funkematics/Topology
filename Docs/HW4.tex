\problem On the previous homework you prove Sutherland 5.1.  Use that result to prove the following.

Let $X$ be a metric space and fix $x_0\in X$.  Show that function $f:X\to\Reals$
defined by $f(x) = d(x,x_0)$ is continuous. \newline
\begin{proof}
  Let $a \in X$ be arbitrary. we want to show $f$ is continuous at $a$. That is we want to show that 
  $$ \forall\varepsilon > 0, \exists\delta > 0 : d_X(x,a) < \delta \implies |f(x) - f(a)| < \varepsilon$$
  Given (Sutherland 5.1) : For any $p, q, r \in X$, $$|d(p,r) - d(q,r)| \leq d(p,q).$$

  Let $\varepsilon > 0$ and choose $\delta = \varepsilon$.

  Let $x \in X$ with $d(x, a) < \delta$.
  
  Then: 
  \begin{align*}
    |f(x) - f(a)| = |d(x,x_0) - d(a,x_0)| \leq d(x,a) < \delta = \varepsilon
  \end{align*}
  Therefore $|f(x) - f(a)| < \varepsilon$. Since $a \in X$ was arbitrary, $f$ is continuous on $X$.
\end{proof}
\problem Sutherland 5.5

Suppose that $x, y$ are distinct points in a metric space $(X,d)$ and let
$\epsilon = d(x,y)/2$. Prove that $B_\epsilon(x)$ and $B_\epsilon(y)$ are disjoint. \newline

\begin{proof}
  \textbf{By Contradiction} For $B_\epsilon(x)$ and $B_\epsilon(y)$ to be disjoint, it must be the case that $B_\epsilon(x) \cap B_\epsilon(y) = \emptyset$.
  Suppose $B_\epsilon(x) \cap B_\epsilon(y)$ is not empty, then there exists $z \in B_\epsilon(x) \cap B_\epsilon(y)$. Unfolding the definition of intersection we get $z \in B_\epsilon(x) = \{p \in X : d(x,p) < \epsilon\}$ and $z \in B_\epsilon(y) = \{ p \in X : d(y, p) < \epsilon\}$. So 
  \begin{align*}
    z \in B_\epsilon(x) \implies d(x, z) < \epsilon \\
    z \in B_\epsilon(y) \implies d(y, z) < \epsilon
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}
